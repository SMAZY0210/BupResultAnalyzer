BUP publishes results as a wall of grade codes. This reads that wall and tells you what it will not: where you actually rank, how tightly the class is packed, and which course did the damage.

Open the analyser Drop in an .xlsx sheet. Nothing to install.

Nothing you upload goes anywhere

The sheet is parsed by your own browser and held in memory for as long as the tab is open. There is no server, no account and no database behind this. Close the tab and it is gone.

That matters here, because a tabulation sheet carries every classmate's name, ID and full grade history. There is also a hide names toggle that swaps names for initials, worth using before you screenshot anything into a group chat.

What it works out

Ten sections, generated from whatever sheet you give it.

  1. The class at a glanceHeadcount, mean, median, mode, highest, lowest, and the pass and promotion breakdown.
  2. Spread and shapeStandard deviation, mean and median absolute deviation, IQR, skew, and a histogram.
  3. Top and bottomHighest and lowest CGPA by name, plus the top ten and bottom ten.
  4. Three bandsLow, mid and high, split by headcount so each holds roughly a third of the class.
  5. Course by courseGrade spread per course, mean grade point, fail and absent rates, and which paper sorted the class hardest.
  6. Odd against even IDsA split that should show nothing, with a proper t-test, which makes it a check on everything above.
  7. Other cutsBy sex, by session and by batch, so repeaters do not get mistaken for a weak intake year.
  8. Semester to semesterMean GPA across semesters, plus who climbed the most and who slipped.
  9. Who needs watchingAnyone below 2.50, short on credits, or carrying an F or AB, with the retake load per course.
  10. Every studentSortable, searchable, with rank and percentile. Exports to CSV.

Using it

Get the sheet

Download the published result as an .xlsx file. Both BUP layouts work, titled or bare.

Drop it in

Drag it onto the page, or click to browse. Several sheets at once is fine, each gets a tab.

Read it

Everything renders immediately. Sort the table, hide names, or pull the whole thing out as CSV.

A tabulation sheet is messier than it looks. Students who sat fewer papers have their course blocks pushed left, retake entries sit in the same list as everyone's core courses, and an absent student at 0.00 will quietly wreck the class mean. All three are handled, and there is a toggle for that last one.
